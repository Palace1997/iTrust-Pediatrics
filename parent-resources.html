Parent Resources

Parents shouldn't feel lost, alone, or helpless navigating their child's mental health. This is a calm place to start, with plain-language guidance and a team ready to help.

You know your child best

No one understands your child the way you do. When something feels off, trust that instinct, you don't need to wait for things to get worse, and you don't need a diagnosis in hand before reaching out. Most of the time, the hardest part is simply starting the conversation.

Signs it may be time for support

Every child has hard days. It's worth a closer look when changes last for weeks and show up in more than one place, like home and school:

  • Big shifts in mood, sleep, appetite, or energy
  • Pulling away from friends, family, or favorite activities
  • Frequent worry, sadness, anger, or meltdowns
  • Trouble at school, with focus, or with friendships
  • Saying things like "what's the point" or being very down on themselves

Simple things that help at home

  • Listen first, validate the feeling before jumping to solutions
  • Keep predictable routines, especially around sleep and screens
  • Name feelings out loud, it helps kids feel understood and less alone
  • Notice and mention the good moments, not just the hard ones
  • Take care of yourself too, supported parents raise steadier kids

Questions we hear a lot

"Is this just a phase?"

Sometimes, yes. The clue is duration and impact: if it's lasted weeks and is getting in the way of daily life, it's worth a conversation, even if it turns out to be nothing.

"Will my child have to be on medication?"

Not necessarily. We lead with therapy and holistic support. Medication is only ever considered carefully, alongside therapy, and only when it's truly the right fit, with you part of every decision.

"What happens at the first visit?"

Mostly listening. We get to know your child and your family, answer your questions, and start shaping a plan together, at a pace that feels right.

How itrust supports parents

We don't just treat the child, we walk alongside the whole family. That looks like clear guidance, parent coaching, and a care team that actually talks to each other, so you never feel like you're piecing it together alone.

When to reach out

If you're reading this and something keeps tugging at you, that's reason enough to reach out. We'll help you figure out the next step together.

If your child is in crisis or danger, call or text 988 (the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or call 911. This page is general information, not a substitute for professional care.
