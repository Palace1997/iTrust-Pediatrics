# itrust Pediatrics — Legal Pages: Attorney Review Checklist

**Purpose:** Five draft web pages were created for the itrust Pediatrics website (a child & adolescent **psychiatric** practice, ages 5–17, based in South Carolina). They are **starting templates only** and must be reviewed and finalized by a licensed healthcare attorney and the practice's HIPAA Privacy Officer before they go live.

**Pages to review:** Privacy Policy · HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices · Terms of Use · Good Faith Estimate · Accessibility Statement.

**How to use this:** Every item below is a question to confirm or a blank to fill. In the draft pages, all fill-in items are **highlighted in gold** and written as `[bracketed placeholders]`.

> This checklist is a plain-language aid for the practice and its counsel. It is **not legal advice** and is not exhaustive — counsel should apply their own judgment and current federal/South Carolina law.

---

## 0. Applies to all five pages (do these once)

- [ ] **Legal entity name** — confirm the exact legal name (e.g., "itrust Pediatrics, LLC") and use it consistently.
- [ ] **Real contact details** — replace the current placeholder address, phone, and email everywhere. *(The site currently shows "123 Wellness Way, Suite 200, Greenville, SC 29601," "864-520-2020," and "hello@itrustpediatrics.com" as placeholders.)*

## 1. Privacy Policy (`privacy.html`)

Governs information collected **through the website** (separate from HIPAA/PHI).

- [ ] **Does the contact form collect PHI?** The form asks for the child's first name, age, and a free-text "tell us about your child and what you need" field — responses could contain protected health information. Counsel should decide how to handle this (e.g., a warning not to include medical details, and/or a Business Associate Agreement with whatever service processes the form).
- [ ] **Form processor / BAA** — once the form is wired to a service (e.g., Formspree, Netlify), confirm whether a Business Associate Agreement is required and in place.
- [ ] **Analytics & cookies** — confirm what is actually running (e.g., Google Analytics, or none) and whether a cookie/consent banner is required.
- [ ] **State privacy laws** — confirm which apply. If the practice provides telehealth to families in other states, additional state privacy laws may be triggered.
- [ ] **Children's privacy (COPPA)** — confirm the language is adequate given the audience is parents/guardians of minors.
- [ ] **Data retention & security** — confirm the described safeguards match actual practice.
- [ ] **Contact for privacy requests** — fill in the correct address/phone/email.

---

## 2. HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices (`hipaa.html`) — highest priority

This is the most regulated document. It must satisfy the federal content requirements (**45 CFR 164.520**) and South Carolina law.

- [ ] **Required content elements** — confirm the Notice contains all federally required elements and the exact required header language.
- [ ] **South Carolina preemption** — where SC law is stricter than HIPAA, confirm the Notice reflects it.
- [ ] **Minor consent & confidentiality** — **critical for a child/adolescent psychiatric practice.** Confirm:
  - [ ] When a minor may consent to their own mental-health/psychiatric treatment under SC law;
  - [ ] When a parent/guardian is (and is not) the minor's "personal representative";
  - [ ] Parental access to a minor's psychiatric records vs. the minor's confidentiality.
- [ ] **Psychotherapy notes** — confirm whether the practice maintains "psychotherapy notes" (a defined HIPAA term) and that the special-authorization language is correct.
- [ ] **Uses/disclosures list** — verify the list of permitted disclosures (public health, abuse/neglect reporting, safety threats, law enforcement, etc.) against current law and practice.
- [ ] **Patient rights & the request process** — confirm each right and describe how patients actually submit requests (and any forms used).
- [ ] **Privacy Officer** — fill in name, title, and contact.
- [ ] **Effective date & revision rights.**
- [ ] **Operational duties (confirm these are in place):**
  - [ ] The Notice is **posted in the office** and available on request;
  - [ ] Patients are **given the Notice** and the practice makes a **good-faith effort to obtain written acknowledgment of receipt**;
  - [ ] Breach-notification procedures exist;
  - [ ] Business Associate Agreements are in place with vendors that touch PHI.

## 4. Good Faith Estimate (`good-faith-estimate.html`)

Federal No Surprises Act notice for uninsured/self-pay patients.

- [ ] **Current CMS model language** — confirm the wording matches the latest CMS standard notice.
- [ ] **Timing rules** — confirm the required delivery timelines (e.g., business-day requirements) and the **$400 dispute threshold**.
- [ ] **Scope** — confirm who must receive a GFE (currently framed for uninsured/self-pay) and your actual process for issuing one.
- [ ] **Self-pay rates** — decide whether to publish transparent rates and add them.
- [ ] **Contact & phone numbers** — confirm the practice contact and the CMS help number.

## 5. Accessibility Statement (`accessibility.html`)

- [ ] **Feedback contact & response time** — fill in and confirm.
