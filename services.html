ADHD/ADD

Attention

Some kids have brilliant, busy minds that just work differently. We look past the "behavior problem" label to understand how your child focuses and learns, then build supports around their strengths.

Signs we watch for

  • Hard time focusing, finishing tasks, or sitting still
  • Big emotions and impulsive moments
  • Struggles with homework, routines, or organization
  • Notes home from teachers about attention

How we help

  • A thorough assessment with input from home and school
  • Behavioral strategies, focus supports, and medication management when appropriate
  • Parent coaching and skill-building support
  • School-support guidance so your child is set up to succeed

Common questions

Is ADHD just a discipline or willpower problem?
No. ADHD is a difference in how the brain manages attention, activity, and impulses, not a matter of trying harder or better parenting. We assess it carefully instead of labeling it a “behavior problem.”
How do you diagnose ADHD?
With a thorough evaluation that gathers your child’s history and input from both home and school, plus standardized rating scales, never a single quick visit.
Does my child have to take medication?
Not necessarily. Medication is one option, used only when it’s the right fit. We start conservatively, involve you in every decision, and pair it with behavioral and school supports.

Anxiety

Anxiety

It's normal for kids to worry about a test or a thunderstorm. Anxiety is different, it's worry that gets so loud it crowds out school, sleep, friendships, and play. We take those worries seriously, with no judgment and no rush.

Signs we watch for

  • Stomachaches or headaches with no clear cause
  • Trouble sleeping, or clinging at drop-off
  • Avoiding school, friends, or once-loved activities
  • Constant "what if" worries that won't switch off

How we help

  • A calm, unhurried assessment of what's really going on
  • Medication management when appropriate, plus proven coping strategies
  • Practical coping tools, mindfulness, breathing, and grounding
  • A family plan, so parents know how to support, not pressure

Common questions

How is anxiety different from normal worry?
All kids worry sometimes. It becomes anxiety when the worry is intense, hard to switch off, and starts crowding out school, sleep, friendships, or play.
Can anxiety show up as physical symptoms?
Yes. Frequent stomachaches, headaches, trouble sleeping, or meltdowns at drop-off are common ways anxiety shows up in kids, even when there’s no medical cause.
Do you treat anxiety with medication or therapy?
We’re a psychiatric practice focused on evaluation and medication management. When therapy would also help, we’ll help you connect with a trusted therapist so both work together.

Depression

Mood

When sadness lingers for weeks and a child stops being themselves, it deserves real attention, not a "they'll grow out of it." We help families spot it early and build a path back to feeling like themselves again.

Signs we watch for

  • Lasting sadness, irritability, or numbness
  • Pulling away from friends and family
  • Changes in sleep, appetite, or energy
  • Losing interest in things they used to love

How we help

  • A compassionate evaluation and, where needed, safety planning
  • A psychiatric treatment plan built around your child, including medication when it helps
  • Practical routines for mood, rest, and daily structure
  • Steady follow-up, we stay with you, and refer if more is needed

Common questions

Isn’t it normal for kids and teens to be moody?
Some ups and downs are normal. But sadness, irritability, or numbness that lasts two weeks or more and changes who your child is deserves real attention, not “they’ll grow out of it.”
What are the warning signs of depression?
Lasting sadness or irritability, pulling away from friends and family, losing interest in things they used to love, and changes in sleep, appetite, or energy.
What should I do if my child mentions self-harm?
Take it seriously and don’t wait. Call or text 988, or 911 in an emergency. Then reach out to us so we can help with next steps and ongoing care.

Behavioral concerns

Behavior

Big behaviors, meltdowns, defiance, anger that comes out of nowhere, are usually a child telling us something they can't yet put into words. We help families understand what's underneath and respond in a way that actually calms things down.

Signs we watch for

  • Frequent meltdowns or outbursts beyond their age
  • Defiance, arguing, or trouble with limits
  • Anger or aggression that worries you
  • Conflict that's straining the whole household

How we help

  • Get to the "why" behind the behavior, not just the surface
  • Emotional-regulation strategies and behavioral supports
  • Parent coaching with calm, consistent strategies
  • Family guidance to ease conflict and rebuild connection

Common questions

Are big behaviors just a sign of bad parenting?
No. Meltdowns, defiance, and anger are usually a child communicating something they can’t yet put into words. We help you understand what’s underneath, with no blame.
When are behaviors a reason to seek help?
When outbursts are beyond what’s typical for their age, keep escalating, or are straining the whole household, it’s worth a professional look.
Do you only prescribe medication for behavior?
No. We start by understanding the “why,” then use emotional-regulation strategies, behavioral supports, and parent coaching, with medication only when it clearly helps.

Mood Disorders

Mood

Mood that swings, lingering irritability, or ups and downs beyond the usual can point to a mood disorder. We assess carefully, then help your child find steadier ground.

Signs we watch for

  • Big mood swings or ups and downs
  • Irritability or anger that lingers
  • Periods of unusually high or very low energy
  • Trouble that comes and goes in cycles

How we help

  • A thorough psychiatric evaluation and diagnosis
  • Medication management when it's the right fit
  • A clear plan with close monitoring over time
  • Coordination with your family and school

Common questions

What is a mood disorder in children?
It’s when mood swings, lingering irritability, or ups and downs go beyond the usual and start affecting daily life. A thorough evaluation is key to getting it right.
How is it different from ordinary moodiness?
Ordinary moods pass. A mood disorder tends to be more intense, last longer, or come and go in cycles, and it gets in the way of school, friendships, or home.
How do you diagnose a mood disorder?
With a thorough psychiatric evaluation of your child’s history and patterns over time, not a snap judgment from a single visit.

Medication Management

Care

Medication management is a core part of what we do. When it's the right fit, our prescribers offer conservative medication support that's closely monitored over time.

Signs we watch for

  • A thorough evaluation before anything is prescribed
  • Conservative, evidence-based prescribing
  • Close monitoring and regular check-ins
  • Coordination with your child's care team and school

How we help

  • Conservative by default, medication only when it clearly helps
  • Clear talk about benefits and trade-offs
  • You and your child are part of every decision
  • Adjusted carefully over time, never rushed

Common questions

Is it safe to give psychiatric medication to children?
When it’s the right fit and carefully managed, medication can be safe and helpful. We prescribe conservatively, start low, and monitor closely.
How do you decide whether medication is needed?
Only after a thorough evaluation. We weigh the benefits and trade-offs with you, and recommend medication only when it clearly helps.
Will my child be on medication forever?
Not necessarily. We adjust carefully over time and regularly revisit whether medication is still the right call.

Family-Centered Treatment

Family

Caring for a child means caring for the whole family. Through psychoeducation, care planning, and guidance from our practitioners, we help parents feel confident supporting their child at home.

Signs we watch for

  • Psychoeducation about your child's diagnosis and needs
  • A clear care plan you help shape
  • Practical guidance from our practitioners for home
  • Support for siblings and the whole family

How we help

  • Less guesswork and overwhelm at home
  • Calmer, more consistent routines
  • Everyone pulling in the same direction
  • Confidence you're not navigating it alone

Common questions

What does “family-centered” actually mean?
It means we care for the whole family, not just the appointment. Through psychoeducation and guidance, we help you feel confident supporting your child at home.
Do parents attend visits?
Yes. Parents and guardians are partners in the plan. How involved you are depends on your child’s age and needs, and we’ll talk through what fits.
Can you help our other kids and our routine?
We offer support for siblings and the whole family, plus practical routines that make home calmer and more consistent.
